The break-even win rate at 3:1 is 25%.

The odds are estimates from a simple model that ignores gaps, news, and intraday touches. They are not predictions.

How it’s calculated

The stop is chosen first. Share count comes from the stop, not the other way around. The target must be at least 3× the distance from entry to stop.

Risk $ = Portfolio × Risk %
Risk per share = |Entry − Stop|
Risk shares = floor(Risk $ ÷ Risk per share)
Cap shares = floor(Portfolio × Cap % ÷ Entry)
Shares = min(Risk shares, Cap shares)
Long target = Entry + 3 × (Entry − Stop)
Short target = Entry − 3 × (Stop − Entry)

Risk defaults to 2% of the portfolio and is hard-capped at 2.5%, leaving a little room for slippage. No single position can be worth more than the cap (25% by default). When the cap binds, you risk less than 2%.

For “no edge” and “expected edge,” odds come from 4,000 seeded Monte Carlo paths of geometric Brownian motion, checked at daily closes:

sd = vol ÷ √252
drift = +edge (long) or −edge (short)
mu = drift ÷ 252 − 0.5 × sd²
S ← S × exp(mu + sd × Z)

Each path is counted as target-first, stop-first, or neither by the end of the time frame. For a backtested win rate, hit target = win rate and hit stop = 1 − win rate.

Expectancy (R) = 3 × P(target) − P(stop)
Expectancy ($) = Expectancy (R) × Dollars at risk
